## Visitor-ready standard
Every district file contains district introduction/history, practical hubs, visitor descriptions for every canonical record, map behavior (point/area/corridor/route/water/protected area/HOLD), things to do, permits or entry logic, access from practical hubs and routing status.

## Province-wide parent systems
1. **Greater Lumbini Area** — one cross-district Buddhist heritage system connecting Lumbini (Rupandehi), Tilaurakot and associated Kapilavastu sites, Devdaha (Rupandehi) and Ramgrama (Nawalparasi West). Do not duplicate the parent circuit as independent district attractions.
2. **Banke National Park** — protected-area parent system; visitor routing must use current official gates and current park rules.
3. **Bardiya National Park** — protected-area parent system with Thakurdwara as the main visitor gateway; park activities remain guide/rule dependent.
4. **Blackbuck conservation landscape** — protected grassland/visitor area around Khairapur; use official access rather than a generic area centroid.
5. **Dhorpatan Hunting Reserve** — shared protected landscape across Eastern Rukum, Baglung and Myagdi. Ordinary reserve visits and regulated hunting permissions are separate products.
6. **Ridi/Ruru Kshetra** — one Kali Gandaki pilgrimage landscape with Palpa and Gulmi district-side access records.
7. **Ramdi / Kali Gandaki** — shared river/pilgrimage crossing; one parent geography, separate safe access nodes.
8. **Lisne Lekh** — shared Pyuthan–Rolpa highland destination with separate approach routes.
9. **Guerrilla Trek** — cross-district historical/cultural trekking route linking Rolpa, Eastern Rukum and adjoining western districts.

## Dynamic data policy
The following are never hard-coded as permanent facts without a dated recheck: protected-area fees, safari products, museum hours, monastery/temple operating rules, festival dates, border/security access, road condition, river levels, boating, commercial adventure operation, active homestays and trail closures.

## Routing safety rules
- Water bodies route to verified shoreline/visitor access, never water centroids.
- National parks/reserves route to official gates or authorized visitor zones, never protected-area centroids.
- Archaeological landscapes use precinct/area geometry; individual gates, pillars, museums and temples can use precise pins after GIS verification.
- Rivers use corridor geometry and named safe access nodes.
- Unverified caves, forts, wetlands, viewpoints and community products remain ROUTING_HOLD.
- Border and infrastructure landscapes require current legal/security access checks.
