### 5. Pashupatinath Temple / Pashupati area

- **District:** Kathmandu
- **Municipality / area:** Kathmandu Metropolitan City, Bagmati River / Gaushala-Pashupati area
- **UNESCO component:** Kathmandu Valley, Pashupati (121bis-006)
- **Coordinates:** 27.710523, 85.348840
- **Coordinate status:** UNESCO component coordinate
- **Visitor fee (source checked 2026-08-08):** Foreign nationals NPR 1,000 per day/per entry; current NTB table states Indian nationals are free; Nepalese free. Recheck before publication.
- **Permit requirement:** No separate travel permit identified; site entrance fee applies to tourists.
- **Religious access restriction:** Nepal Tourism Board states the main temple complex is open only to Hindus. Non-Hindus can observe from terraces across the Bagmati River to the east.
- **Photography / conduct:** NTB states photography is prohibited in the main temple complex and leather items including shoes, belts and cameras are forbidden within the temple complex.
- **Access context:** NTB places the temple about 5 km east of the city center; public transport/taxis can reach Gaushala followed by a short walk.
- **Primary sources:**

- **Coordinates:** 27.742222, 85.364167

- **Name note:** The monastery itself uses the spelling “Kopan”; Nepal Tourism Board currently lists the destination as “Kapan Monastery.” Preserve both spellings for search and aliases.
- **Visitor context:** Nepal Tourism Board describes it as a Gelug/“yellow hat” Tibetan Buddhist monastery on a hill north of Boudhanath. The monastery says it was established in the late 1960s as a home for Himalayan monks and nuns and for foreigners seeking Buddhist teachings.
