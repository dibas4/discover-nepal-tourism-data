## 18. Narayanhiti Palace Museum

- **District:** Kathmandu
- **Municipality / area:** Kathmandu Metropolitan City, Durbar Marg
- **Destination type:** Former royal palace / history museum
- **Tourism context:** Nepal Tourism Board identifies Narayanhiti as the former residence of Nepal's Shah kings and now a museum in central Kathmandu.
- **Name origin:** NTB explains that the name derives from the Narayan temple within the palace grounds and the nearby hiti (water feature/spout).
- **Museum organization:** NTB currently describes three principal complexes: Tribhuwan Sadan, Shree Sadan and the main palace.
- **Architecture:** NTB states that architect Benjamin Polk designed the main palace in 1963 and that it was completed in 1969, combining Western and Nepali architectural elements.
- **Interior context:** NTB states that the main palace contains 52 rooms named after districts of Nepal.
- **Historical context:** Tribhuwan Sadan is associated with the 2001 royal massacre. Traveler-facing copy should distinguish museum interpretation and established facts from speculation surrounding the event.
- **Coordinates:** Exact public entrance point should be captured separately from the palace-complex centroid during GIS QA.
- **Entry fee / hours:** No sufficiently reliable current official tariff/hour table was found in this research pass. Do not use travel-blog figures as authoritative; recheck with the museum/government immediately before publication.
- **Primary sources:**

  - https://tourism.gov.np/
- **Verification status:** Museum identity, location context and major historical/architectural details verified from NTB; current tariff/hours pending.
