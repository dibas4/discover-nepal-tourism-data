## Coverage notes

- Simraungadh is divided into the medieval capital landscape, fortifications, palace/Ranivas area, Kankali Temple and living heritage town because they are different tourism products.
- Archaeological objects reportedly found at Simraungadh are not treated as separate attractions unless their current museum or on-site display location is confirmed.
- Parsa National Park crosses Bara, Parsa and Makwanpur; this record covers only the Bara sector.
- Halkhoria Daha, Tangiya Basti and unnamed Chure viewpoints remain unsuitable for detailed public pages until coordinates, access and present condition are confirmed.
- River corridors are listed as landscapes, not automatically as safe swimming or picnic sites.

## Sources and evidence used

- Government of Nepal protected-area framework and Department of National Parks and Wildlife Conservation references for Parsa National Park.
- Archaeological research and heritage references for the medieval city, ramparts, ditches and sculpture landscape of Simraungadh.
- Mahagadhimai and Simraungadh local-government context for Gadhimai, Bariyarpur and the heritage municipality.
- District and local-level references for Bara’s rivers, forests, municipalities and Chure landscape.
