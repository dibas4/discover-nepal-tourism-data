## 21. Malekhu river and bazaar area

**Disposition:** ESTABLISHED — retain as a highway/river visitor stop, not a single monument POI.

- Malekhu is an established Prithvi Highway settlement/stop in Dhading associated with the Malekhu Khola–Trishuli corridor.
- Its tourism identity is primarily a gateway/highway-stop and food identity, especially fish-food tourism, rather than a bounded attraction.
- Keep river scenery, bazaar/service functions and food identity under one area record unless later field research identifies a distinct public riverfront attraction.

**Planner treatment:** service/gateway area; current restaurants, parking, road disruption and river access are dynamic and should not be hard-coded from research-stage evidence.

## 22. Chitwan Chepang Hill Trail — Dhading sector

**Disposition:** ESTABLISHED — strong official tourism evidence.

- Nepal Tourism Board states that the Chitwan Chepang Hill Trail begins at Hugdi, between Mugling and Krishna Bhir in Dhading district, and ends at Shaktikhor.
- NTB describes the trail as a cultural and sightseeing route featuring Chepang and other local communities, birdwatching, natural attractions, homestays/community guest houses, and mid-hill forest scenery.
- This confirms a genuine Dhading trail sector; do not model the entire multi-district trail as if it lies inside Dhading.

**Planner treatment:** route/corridor object beginning at the verified Hugdi gateway. Exact route geometry, current homestays, trail condition and seasonal access need live/local verification.

**Primary source:** Nepal Tourism Board — https://ntb.gov.np/en/chepang-hill-trail

## 23. Netrawati–Dabjong heritage landscape

**Disposition:** ESTABLISHED AS A CULTURAL/HERITAGE LANDSCAPE; exact component-site geometry still requires local verification.

- Netrawati Dabjong Rural Municipality officially explains that its name combines the Netrawati river with Dabjong Durbar, historically associated with the Waiba king.
- This is strong local-government evidence that Dabjong is not an invented tourism label and that it forms part of the municipality's historical identity.
- The broader record should represent the fort/durbar, settlements and cultural landscape together, while avoiding invented boundaries.

**Planner treatment:** heritage landscape/area object; publish conservative historical context, but require local GIS/ward evidence before drawing a polygon or detailed heritage walking route.

**Primary source:** Netrawati Dabjong Rural Municipality — https://netrawatidabjongmun.gov.np/

## 24. Dabjong Durbar / fort area

**Disposition:** ESTABLISHED HISTORICAL IDENTITY, HOLD FOR PRECISE PUBLIC POI DETAILS.

- The municipality's own introduction states that the local-government name derives partly from Dabjong Durbar and links it historically with the Waiba king.
- This is enough to retain the record as a real historical/fort-palace area.
- Current evidence in this pass is not sufficient to claim the exact surviving structures, entrance point, visitor facilities, opening hours or a precise public pin.

**Planner treatment:** research-backed heritage record, but no precise navigation target until local/field verification confirms surviving remains and access.

**Primary source:** Netrawati Dabjong Rural Municipality — https://netrawatidabjongmun.gov.np/

## 25. Jyamrung Durbar area

**Disposition:** HOLD / NEEDS STRONGER SITE-LEVEL VERIFICATION.

- The canonical inventory places the historic Jyamrung Durbar area in Nilkantha Municipality and treats it as a historic hilltop palace/fort landscape.
- This research pass did not recover sufficiently strong authoritative site-level evidence to safely publish exact coordinates, surviving structures, access, ownership, or visitor status.
- Do not promote weak travel-blog/map repetition into verified heritage facts.

**Planner treatment:** retain in the research inventory but block detailed public guide/navigation until Nilkantha Municipality, archaeology/heritage documentation, mapped government evidence, or field verification establishes the exact site.
